# Agent 1 — General Biography (raw findings)

> Commander Sir Gerald Francis Talbot KCVO, CMG, OBE (1881–1945)

## Vital statistics
- **Full name:** Gerald Francis Talbot; styled "Sir" after KCVO (1922).
- **Born:** 21 August 1881 (birthplace unconfirmed; probable Hertfordshire/England, Chetwynd-Talbot milieu).
- **Died:** 17 April 1945, aged 63 (place & cause unknown in open sources).
- **Burial:** not located online.

## Family & lineage
- **Father:** Lt-Col Gerald Francis Talbot (1848–1904). Served in the **2nd Prussian Dragoon Guards**; wrote *Analysis of the Organization of the Prussian Army*. JP for Essex & Kent; Lieutenant of the City of London. (NB: a genuine German military connection in the family.)
- **Mother:** Henrietta Clarissa Noyes Bradhurst (American — Bradhurst/Noyes, New York associations).
- **Grandfather:** Rev. Hon. William Whitworth Chetwynd-Talbot (1814–1888), son of **Charles Chetwynd-Talbot, 2nd Earl Talbot**; Rector of Bishop's Hatfield.
- **Lineage:** cadet branch of the Chetwynd-Talbots; cousins to the **Earls of Shrewsbury** (premier earldom; descends from John Talbot, 1st Earl, of the Hundred Years' War). Gerald = great-grandnephew of the 18th Earl of Shrewsbury. Not in line of succession.
- **Siblings:** Henry Augustus (1871–1875), Victor Adelbert (1872–1932), Bertram William Chetwynd (1876–1941), Stafford Cecil (1880–1950), **Gerald Francis (1881–1945)**, Louise Victoria Gisela (1885–1950).

## Naval career
- Served in the **Royal Naval Volunteer Reserve (RNVR)** — civilian volunteer branch (often a cover for intelligence work).
- **23 Aug 1917:** Acting Commander RNVR (per Dreadnought Project; page 403-blocked, datum via snippets). Eastern Mediterranean / possibly Constantinople.
- **OBE** before June 1920 (WWI service).
- **By mid-1920:** Naval Attaché to HM Legation, Athens. Likely from ~1919 (possibly linked to the Kelly Naval Mission, 1919–21). Was "former" attaché by Nov 1922.

## The 1922 Prince Andrew rescue (summary)
- Greek Revolutionary Committee seized power Sept 1922 after the Asia Minor catastrophe; "Trial of the Six" executions Nov 1922. Prince Andrew of Greece (son of King George I; father of infant Prince Philip) court-martialled.
- **Venizelos** (Talbot's friend) and **Lord Curzon** sent Talbot to Athens; arrived **30 Nov 1922** (too late to save the Six).
- Negotiated with Plastiras, Gonatas, Pangalos, Rentis: Andrew to be sentenced then pardoned into Talbot's custody.
- Court-martial **3 Dec 1922** → exile for life. Evacuated via **HMS Calypso** from Phaleron Bay; collected Princess Alice & children (incl. ~18-month-old **Prince Philip** in an orange-box cot) at Corfu; reached **Brindisi 5 Dec 1922**.
- KCVO awarded for the service.

## "Spy" reputation
- RNVR + Eastern Med posting + Naval Attaché (intrinsically intelligence) + Venizelos friendship + secret 1922 mission = strong intelligence profile. No open SIS/MI6 records (era's records closed). "Goodbye Anatolia" paper inflates him to "Admiral Lord Gerald Talbot" (error).
